# Barral Experience

Create a premium, highly interactive, mobile-responsive website for my Indian beverage brand, “BARRAL.”

IMPORTANT CREATIVE DIRECTION

The website should feel like a cinematic digital exhibition—not a conventional beverage e-commerce website.

Use the attached Instagram reel only as inspiration for its:

Full-screen visual storytelling

Editorial composition

Oversized serif typography

Scroll-driven transitions

Layered images

Cinematic pacing

Gallery-like presentation

Dramatic use of negative space

Do not copy the exact Renaissance paintings, imagery, layout or content from the reference. Reinterpret the experience using Barral’s own brand world, mascot, product and personality.

BRAND INFORMATION

Brand name: BARRAL
Brand mascot: Mr. Barral
Category: Prepare-to-Drink beverage
Product format: Double-wall paper cup, dome lid, straw and instant mixer sachet
Preparation: Add the mixer, add cold water, close and stir
Preparation time: Approximately 10–15 seconds
Primary audience: Gen Z and young working consumers in India
Product positioning: A beverage experience—not another ready-made commodity
Visual personality: Bold, rebellious, mysterious, premium, collectible and slightly arrogant
Primary colours: Black, off-white and Barral red
Supporting colours: Use flavour-specific accent colours
Primary CTA: Pre-Order Barral

CORE WEBSITE IDEA

Present Barral as an interactive “Gallery of a New Drinking Behaviour.”

The visitor should feel like they are moving through a cinematic story. Each scroll should reveal a new scene, idea or product interaction.

The experience must feel artistic and memorable while keeping the pre-order journey clear and easy.

TECHNICAL FOUNDATION

Build the website using React and TypeScript.

Use:

GSAP

GSAP ScrollTrigger

Framer Motion

Lenis smooth scrolling, if it improves the experience

Responsive image loading

Lazy loading

WebP or AVIF images

WebM video where appropriate

Keep all motion smooth and premium. Avoid excessive effects that make the website difficult to use.

Create simplified animation behaviour for mobile devices.

Respect “prefers-reduced-motion” accessibility settings.

Do not use generic template sections, colourful gradients, glassmorphism, SaaS-style cards, excessive rounded boxes or random stock photographs.

TYPOGRAPHY

Use an editorial typography system:

Large, dramatic serif font for headlines

Clean sans-serif font for descriptions, navigation and buttons

Oversized typography should occasionally become part of the visual composition

Headlines can overlap imagery carefully, but must remain readable

Use a font pairing similar to:

Cormorant Garamond or Bodoni Moda for headings

Inter, Manrope or Neue Montreal-style sans serif for interface text

SCENE 1 — CINEMATIC OPENING

Create a full-screen opening scene with a black background.

Display Mr. Barral and the Barral cup as the central visual composition. They should appear like the subjects of a contemporary editorial painting.

Show the following headline:

“A COLD DRINK SHOULD BE MORE THAN A COMMODITY.”

Below it, display:

“Meet Barral—the drink that begins when you make it.”

Add two buttons:

Enter the Experience

Pre-Order Barral

Animate the headline, Mr. Barral and the cup into view using slow masked reveals, subtle scale changes and controlled lighting.

Include a small scrolling indicator:

“Scroll to prepare”

The hero must immediately communicate that Barral is a beverage product, not a fashion or art website.

SCENE 2 — CATEGORY REVEAL

Create a pinned scroll section.

Initially show only the finished Barral cup.

As the visitor scrolls, visually separate the product into four floating components:

Barral Cup

Instant Mixer

Cold Water

10–15 Seconds

Connect the components using thin editorial lines and small labels.

Headline:

“NOT READY TO DRINK.
READY WHEN YOU ARE.”

Supporting text:

“Barral transforms a sealed cup, an instant mixer and cold water into a fresh beverage experience—wherever the moment finds you.”

The product components should slowly assemble again into the completed Barral cup.

SCENE 3 — INTERACTIVE PREPARATION

Create a cinematic four-step preparation sequence controlled by scrolling.

Step 01
Visual: Mixer sachet entering the frame
Text: “TEAR”

Step 02
Visual: Mixer being poured into the cup
Text: “POUR”

Step 03
Visual: Cold water filling the cup
Text: “ADD WATER”

Step 04
Visual: Dome lid closing and the drink being stirred
Text: “STIR. SIP. BARRAL.”

End the sequence with the liquid changing into the selected flavour colour.

Add the line:

“Your drink. Your ritual. Your moment.”

If final animation assets are not yet available, build the section with replaceable image placeholders and clean component structure. Do not generate unrealistic product packaging.

SCENE 4 — FLAVOUR GALLERY

Create four full-screen flavour environments.

Each flavour should feel like a different emotional world rather than a conventional product card.

FLAVOUR 01

Name: REFRESHING
Moment: Morning and post-breakfast refreshment
Visual world: Ice, citrus atmosphere, wind and crisp daylight
Copy: “RESET THE MORNING.”

FLAVOUR 02

Name: CAFFEINE
Moment: Afternoon energy and post-lunch recovery
Visual world: Deep brown, electric red, controlled energy and motion
Copy: “WAKE UP THE SECOND HALF.”

FLAVOUR 03

Name: PANIPURI
Moment: Tangy, spicy and nostalgic craving
Visual world: Bold red, spice particles, street-culture energy and modern Indian attitude
Copy: “STREET FLAVOUR. DIFFERENT FORM.”

FLAVOUR 04

Name: BROWNIE HAZELNUT
Moment: Indulgence and craving satisfaction
Visual world: Cocoa textures, hazelnut tones and premium dessert lighting
Copy: “DRINK THE CRAVING.”

As the user scrolls, transition between flavour environments using colour, texture and liquid movement.

Do not show these flavours as four ordinary cards in a grid.

Add a “Discover This Flavour” interaction to each flavour.

SCENE 5 — THE 24 DESIGNS

Create a dark gallery containing 24 collectible Barral cup designs.

Headline:

“24 DESIGNS.
ONE FOR EVERY VERSION OF YOU.”

Supporting copy:

“Your pack does not just carry drinks. It carries personalities, moods and stories.”

Present the cups as rotating gallery exhibits.

Possible interaction:

Horizontal scroll on desktop

Swipeable gallery on mobile

Hover to rotate the cup slightly

Click to view the complete 360-degree design

Display the design number, name and personality

Initially use placeholders if all 24 cup designs have not been uploaded. Every placeholder must be easy to replace later.

Add:

“Every pack contains a surprise selection of designs.”

Create a dramatic black-and-white scene featuring Mr. Barral.

ANOTHER BEVERAGE.”

MOBILE EXPERIENCE

The mobile version is extremely important.

For mobile:

Maintain the cinematic storytelling

Reduce heavy parallax

Use shorter pinned sections

Replace hover interactions with tap or swipe

Keep text readable

Avoid overlapping critical content

Keep the Pre-Order button easily accessible

Optimise animations for mid-range Indian smartphones

Ensure the site works on slower mobile connections

Do not autoplay large videos over mobile data unless optimised

BUILD PROCESS

Start by building the complete homepage structure, design system and responsive layout.

Then implement the opening scene and category-reveal animation.

After that, create the preparation sequence, flavour gallery, collectible cup gallery and pre-order section.

Use reusable components and keep all product information in a central data file so flavours, prices, cup designs and pack details can be updated easily.

Before writing the final implementation, ask me for any missing assets or information that would materially affect the visual result.

The final website should feel like:

“A rebellious Indian beverage brand presented as an interactive digital exhibition.”

It must create curiosity, communicate the product within the first screen, explain the Prepare-to-Drink process clearly and guide the visitor naturally toward pre-ordering.
